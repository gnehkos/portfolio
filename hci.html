About LanKrong

A mobile application solving public transit friction for daily bus commuters in Phnom Penh. Real-time tracking, route planning, and digital ticketing in one place.

The Problem

Bus riders in Phnom Penh have no reliable way to check routes, arrival times, or purchase tickets digitally. Most commuters rely on word of mouth or guesswork, leading to long wait times and missed buses. Our team set out to design a solution that removes this friction entirely.

User Pain Points

01

No route visibility. Commuters had no way to see live bus locations or estimated arrival times, leading to long unpredictable waits.

02

Cash-only ticketing. Riders often lacked exact change, causing delays at boarding and friction with drivers.

03

Confusing route names. Route numbers and directions were unclear to first-time or occasional riders, especially tourists and students.

04

No accessibility considerations. The existing system had no accommodations for elderly or visually impaired commuters.

Design Process

We conducted field research and interviews with daily commuters to understand real pain points before sketching a single screen. From there we built low-fidelity paper wireframes, tested early concepts with users, then translated validated flows into a high-fidelity interactive Figma prototype with a full design system.

Reflection

This project was my first time conducting real user interviews. I was surprised how different the actual user pain points were from what I initially assumed. The usability testing phase taught me that even small friction in a UI flow, like an unclear confirmation screen, causes users to hesitate or drop off entirely. Designing for accessibility also forced me to think beyond my own experience as a user.

Week 5–6 — Information Architecture & Low-Fi Wireframes

Week 05–06
Submitted
  • App sitemap and navigation structure
  • Paper wireframes for 6 key screens
  • Annotated wireframe walkthrough document
Notes & Reflections
  • Paper wireframes are faster to iterate than digital, do this first
  • Navigation depth beyond 3 levels confused test participants
  • Question: How do we decide when low-fi is ready for high-fi?
  • Next: Transfer to Figma and build component library

Week 7–9 — High-Fidelity Figma Prototype

Week 07–09
Submitted
  • Full Figma component library (buttons, inputs, cards, nav)
  • High-fidelity prototype with interactive flows
  • Design token documentation (colors, typography, spacing)
Notes & Reflections
  • Consistent spacing using an 8pt grid made everything cleaner
  • Auto layout in Figma saved hours of manual resizing
  • Accessibility contrast ratios matter more than I initially thought
  • Next: Recruit participants for usability testing

Week 10–12 — Usability Testing & Heuristic Evaluation

Week 10–12
Submitted
  • Usability test scripts and task scenarios
  • Test results report (5 participants, 3 tasks each)
  • Heuristic evaluation against Nielsen's 10 principles
  • Iterated prototype v2 with fixes applied
Notes & Reflections
  • Users struggled with the ticket confirmation screen, fixed visibility of system status
  • Think-aloud protocol gives richer data than post-task surveys alone
  • 5 users is enough to catch 85% of usability issues (Nielsen's rule)
  • Next: Final presentation and portfolio submission
